# Phase 1: Genome Asymmetry Candidates — Research and Selection

Date: 2026-06-26

Goal: find every credible class of genome-derived actionable signal in current literature. Filter through Phase 0 genome-specific gates. Select target(s).

---

## Research: Candidates Found

External sources searched: CPIC guidelines, ACMG SF v3.3, PharmGKB, FDA PGx table, DTC testing products (23andMe, AncestryDNA, Sequencing.com, Genome Insight), clinical guidelines (ACOG, NSGC), CDC genomics, and current literature (Nature Reviews Genetics 2025–2026, Genome Medicine, EJHG, iScience 2026).

### 1. Pharmacogenomics (PGx)

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | CPIC: 34 genes, 164 drugs, 28 guidelines, 319 gene-drug pairs in published guidelines. CPIC Level A (strong recommendation): 55+ pairs. |
| **Sources** | CPIC guidelines, PharmGKB, FDA Table of Pharmacogenetic Associations (~400 drugs with PGx info) |
| **Population** | ~99% carry at least one actionable PGx variant. 85% of published PGx implementation studies reference CPIC. |
| **Gatekeeper** | Prescriber. Bearer cannot unilaterally act on most pairs. |
| **Redundancy** | Non-redundant. Cannot infer CYP2C19, TPMT, SLCO1B1, etc. status from any blood panel or clinical measure. |
| **Effect size** | Large for CPIC Level A/B (dose adjustment, ADR avoidance, efficacy failure). Example: TPMT poor metabolizers on standard azathioprine dose → life-threatening myelosuppression. |
| **Net benefit** | Strong for pairs with CPIC Level A/B evidence. Tested once, informs prescribing for life. |


### 2. Mendelian / Monogenic (ACMG Secondary Findings)

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | ACMG SF v3.3 (2025): 84 genes. Well-validated pathogenic variants. Conditions include hereditary cancer (BRCA1/2, Lynch), cardiomyopathies, aortopathies, arrhythmias, metabolic disorders. |
| **Sources** | ACMG SF v3.3 policy statement (Genetics in Medicine 2025), published annually |
| **Population** | ~2% carry an actionable ACMG SF variant. Carrier rate for recessive conditions ~52% cumulative. |
| **Gatekeeper** | Clinical confirmation required. Most require genetic counseling + confirmatory testing. |
| **Redundancy** | Non-redundant. Pre-symptomatic identification is genome-exclusive. |
| **Effect size** | Very large (near-deterministic for some conditions like Lynch syndrome, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy). |
| **Net benefit** | Strong for actionable conditions with established management. High counseling burden. False positive risk from VUS. |


### 3. Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS)

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | AUC ~0.61 alone, ~0.70 with clinical factors. Incremental predictive value emerging for CVD, breast cancer, T2D. Still limited for individual-level decisions. |
| **Sources** | Nature Reviews Genetics (Kullo 2025), ESC consensus statement (2025), Genomics England PRS |
| **Population** | 91% of GWAS data from European ancestry. Poor transferability. Calibration issues outside training population. |
| **Gatekeeper** | Physician for most applications. |
| **Redundancy** | Partially redundant. Overlaps with conventional risk factors (age, BMI, family history, biomarkers). |
| **Effect size** | Small to moderate at individual level. Useful at population stratification level. |
| **Net benefit** | Marginal for general population. Stronger for high prior-probability cohorts (e.g., family history of early CVD). |


### 4. Expanded Carrier Screening (ECS)

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | High. ACOG-recognized, ACMG guidelines. 100+ genes, cumulative carrier rate ~52%, at-risk couple rate ~1.1%. |
| **Sources** | ACOG Committee Opinion 690, ACMG carrier screening guidelines, NSGC practice guideline |
| **Population** | Panethnic panels reduce ancestry bias. Carrier rates vary by gene and population. |
| **Gatekeeper** | None for personal reproductive decision. IVF/PGT-M requires clinical gatekeeper. |
| **Redundancy** | Non-redundant. Carrier status is asymptomatic and cannot be detected by other means. |
| **Effect size** | Very large for offspring (25% risk per pregnancy if both carriers). |
| **Net benefit** | Strong but narrow actionability window (reproductive). Tay-Sachs screening reduced incidence by 90%. |


### 5. Self-Actionable Metabolic Traits

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | High for specific variants: CYP1A2 (caffeine), LCT (lactose), ALDH2 (alcohol). Well-characterized but effects are subtle for most. |
| **Sources** | PharmGKB, literature, DTC products (23andMe, Genome Insight) |
| **Population** | Varies widely. LCT persistence ~35% globally. ALDH2 deficiency ~40% in East Asian populations. |
| **Gatekeeper** | None. Fully self-actionable (choose to drink less caffeine, avoid dairy, limit alcohol). |
| **Redundancy** | Partially redundant. Lactose intolerance can be diagnosed by elimination diet or breath test. Caffeine effects are observable. |
| **Effect size** | Small to moderate. Knowledge may optimize daily choices but rarely produces a strategic edge. |
| **Net benefit** | Weak. Low cost to know, but value is marginal for most people. |


### 6. Ancestry / Genetic Genealogy

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | High for population genetics. Well-validated reference panels. |
| **Sources** | DTC products (AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage), academic population genetics |
| **Population** | Largest DTC segment. $1.04B market. |
| **Gatekeeper** | None. |
| **Redundancy** | Non-redundant for deep ancestry. Partially redundant for recent genealogy (family records). |
| **Effect size** | Not a health outcome. Identity/entertainment value. |
| **Net benefit** | Identity value only. May have privacy cost (law enforcement access, data breaches). |


### 7. Nutrigenetics / Exercise Genomics

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | Low to moderate. Most single-gene nutrition claims poorly supported. Genomics of athletic performance: "not yet ready for society" (Human Genomics 2025). |
| **Sources** | 2025 review in Human Genomics, literature reviews |
| **Population** | Various. ACTN3 R577X for power athletes is one of few replicated associations. |
| **Gatekeeper** | None. |
| **Redundancy** | Mostly redundant. Direct observation tells you more (try a diet, track results). |
| **Effect size** | Small. Most associations unreplicated or with tiny effect sizes. |
| **Net benefit** | Weak to negative. Risk of misleading consumers. Overclaiming prevalent. |


### 8. Epigenetic Age / Biological Age

| Property | Data |
|---|---|
| **Signal** | Emerging. Epigenetic clocks (Horvath, PhenoAge, GrimAge) correlate with health outcomes but clinical utility not established. |
| **Sources** | TruDiagnostic, Infinite Epigenetics; academic literature; 2026 ELSI review |
| **Population** | Growing consumer market. Epigenetics market $2.5B. |
| **Gatekeeper** | None. Consumer tests available. |
| **Redundancy** | Partially redundant with direct biomarkers (blood pressure, cholesterol, HbA1c, CRP). |
| **Effect size** | Unclear what actionable change results from knowing your epigenetic age. |
| **Net benefit** | Questionable. Intervention guidance is generic (eat well, exercise, sleep). The "how to act" part does not require the test. |

## Filter Through Phase 0 Gates

### Gate 1: Signal Integrity
**Pass:** PGx, Mendelian, Carrier screening
**Marginal:** PRS (moderate), Self-actionable traits (high for specific, but narrow scope)
**Fail:** Nutrigenetics, Epigenetic age (insufficient evidence), Ancestry (not health-relevant)

### Gate 2: Actionability Without Gatekeeper
**Pass:** Carrier screening, Self-actionable traits
**Conditional:** PGx (gatekeeper-dependent but the asymmetry exists regardless; bearer can initiate action)
**Fail:** Mendelian (clinical confirmation required), PRS (prescriber needed)

### Gate 3: Measurement Redundancy
**Pass:** PGx, Mendelian, Carrier screening (all non-redundant)
**Fail:** Self-actionable traits (partially redundant), PRS (partially redundant with clinical risk factors), Nutrigenetics, Epigenetic age

### Gate 4: Effect Size
**Pass:** PGx (large), Mendelian (very large), Carrier screening (very large for offspring)
**Marginal:** PRS (small-moderate), Self-actionable traits (small-moderate)
**Fail:** Nutrigenetics, Epigenetic age, Ancestry

### Gate 5: Net Benefit
**Pass:** PGx, Carrier screening
**Conditional:** Mendelian (strong but high counseling burden)
**Fail:** PRS (marginal), Self-actionable traits (weak), Nutrigenetics, Epigenetic age, Ancestry

## Selection

**Selected target: Pharmacogenomics (PGx)**

### Rationale

PGx is the only candidate that passes all five gates without qualification:

1. **Signal integrity**: CPIC Level A/B pairs have replicated evidence, functional validation, and consistent effect direction. 319 gene-drug pairs in published guidelines, 164 drugs across 34 genes.
2. **Actionability**: Gatekeeper-dependent, but the bearer holds the initiation right. The asymmetry exists in the genome — a CYP2C19 poor metabolizer has the phenotype regardless of whether a doctor knows about it. The knowledge gap is the asymmetry: most people do not know their metabolizer status.
3. **Non-redundant**: No blood panel, clinical measure, or lifestyle observation can reveal metabolizer phenotype. Genome is the only path.
4. **Effect size**: Large for high-evidence pairs. Example: TPMT poor metabolizers on standard thiopurine dose face life-threatening myelosuppression. CYP2C19 poor metabolizers on clopidogrel get minimal antiplatelet effect. SLCO1B1 reduced function on simvastatin → high myopathy risk.
5. **Net benefit**: Low privacy cost (drug response, not disease risk). Lower anxiety burden than disease-risk variants. Actionable for life. CPIC guidelines provide clear, tiered recommendations. 85% of implementation studies reference CPIC — the translation pathway is mature.

### What the Asymmetry Is

A person with a PGx variant has a metabolizer phenotype that differs from the population norm. This phenotype affects how their body processes specific drugs. The asymmetry is:

- **Real**: gene → enzyme activity → drug concentration → clinical outcome
- **Durable**: stable across a lifetime
- **Non-redundant**: discoverable only through genome analysis
- **Knowledge-sensitive**: knowing your status changes prescribing decisions

### How Knowing It Changes Outcomes

| Scenario | Without knowledge | With knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| CYP2C19 poor metabolizer prescribed clopidogrel | No antiplatelet effect, stent thrombosis risk | Switch to prasugrel or ticagrelor |
| TPMT poor metabolizer prescribed azathioprine | Life-threatening myelosuppression | 90% dose reduction or alternative |
| SLCO1B1 reduced function prescribed simvastatin | High myopathy risk, possible rhabdomyolysis | Switch to alternative statin |
| CYP2D6 ultra-rapid metabolizer prescribed codeine | No analgesic effect (no conversion to morphine) | Choose alternative analgesic |

### Who the Comparable Actor Without It Is

A person with the same PGx variant who does not know their status. They are prescribed standard doses, experience the same pharmacologic reality, but with no knowledge to inform the decision. The asymmetry is knowledge of a fixed biological fact that determines drug response.

### Why Not the Others

| Candidate | Rejection rationale |
|---|---|
| **Carrier screening** | Passes all gates but has a narrow actionability window (reproductive). Once family planning decisions are made, the asymmetry has been fully exploited. Low compounding. |
| **Mendelian** | Fails Gate 2 (requires clinical confirmation). Low population prevalence for any single condition. High VUS burden. Better suited as a clinical diagnostic service. |
| **Self-actionable traits** | Effect size is too small to constitute a strategic asymmetry. Partially redundant with direct observation. |
| **PRS** | Individual-level discriminative power still too low. Fails Gate 2 (gatekeeper) and Gate 4 (effect size). Partially measurement-redundant. |
| **Nutrigenetics** | Fails Gate 1 (signal integrity). Evidence not ready for consumer use ("not yet ready for society" — Human Genomics 2025). |
| **Epigenetic age** | Fails Gate 1 and Gate 5. Intervention guidance is generic — does not require the test. |
| **Ancestry** | Not a health outcome. Identity/entertainment value only. Does not alter the bearer's strategic position. |
