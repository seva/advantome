# Phase 0: What Is Unfair Advantage?

Date: 2026-06-26

Goal: define the target concept before applying it to genome research.

## Working Definition

An unfair advantage is an asymmetry that lets one actor get better results than comparable actors with less effort, lower cost, faster feedback, lower risk, or higher compounding.

It is not merely "something useful." It has to be asymmetric.

## Minimum Criteria

An advantage is unfair when it has most of these properties:

1. **Valuable**: improves a meaningful outcome.
2. **Rare or underused**: not widely held or not widely exploited by the relevant comparison group.
3. **Hard to copy**: difficult to imitate because of timing, context, tacit knowledge, relationships, physiology, capital, credibility, or accumulated practice.
4. **Exploitable**: the person can actually use it.
5. **Compounding**: produces increasing returns through repeated use.
6. **Context-fit**: matters in the arena being played.
7. **Legible enough to act on**: can be converted into choices, systems, positioning, or experiments.

This is close to the strategic-management idea behind resource-based advantage: resources matter when they are valuable, rare, hard to imitate, and not easily substituted. It also overlaps with core competence theory: durable advantage often comes from combinations of skills, learning, and assets rather than one isolated trait.

## What It Is Not

Not unfair advantage:

- A generic best practice.
- A health improvement everyone should do.
- A genetic trait with no action path.
- A metric improvement with no strategic consequence.
- A temporary trick that disappears once noticed.
- A story that explains identity but changes no decision.
- An advantage that cannot be exploited because the arena is wrong.

## Main Sources Of Unfair Advantage

| Source | Mechanism | Example pattern | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information asymmetry | Knowing something relevant before others or seeing a pattern others miss. | Better map of a market, body, workflow, or opportunity. | Medium if others can learn it; high if tied to tacit judgment. |
| Capability asymmetry | Being able to do something others cannot do as well. | Rare skill stack, speed of learning, technical taste, execution quality. | High if practiced and compounded. |
| Position asymmetry | Being located where opportunity, trust, access, or timing is better. | Network location, institutional role, geographic access, timing. | Medium to high. |
| Resource asymmetry | Having capital, tools, data, time, health, reputation, or infrastructure others lack. | Proprietary dataset, unusual spare time, cash buffer, computing leverage. | Depends on imitability. |
| Taste / judgment asymmetry | Choosing better problems, people, markets, or tradeoffs. | Seeing what matters before it is obvious. | High if hard to teach. |
| Feedback asymmetry | Learning faster because feedback loops are shorter or cleaner. | Tight experiment loops, better instrumentation, direct user/body/work output data. | High if maintained. |
| Constraint asymmetry | Having fewer binding constraints than peers. | Better energy, lower fixed costs, fewer obligations, higher recovery capacity. | Medium; often fragile. |
| Narrative / credibility asymmetry | Others grant attention, trust, or access faster. | Credentials, track record, status, identity-market fit. | High if reputation compounds. |
| System design asymmetry | Better routines, defaults, automation, or environment. | A workflow that produces more output with less decision cost. | Medium; copyable if visible. |

## Ethical Boundary

"Unfair" can mean either:

- **Strategically asymmetric**: legal, ethical, and simply hard for others to copy.
- **Procedurally unfair**: based on deception, privileged non-public access, coercion, or rule-breaking.

Advantome should target the first and reject the second.

## RAROC 2x2 For Advantage Types

Axes:

- X-axis: exploitability now
- Y-axis: durability / compounding potential

### High Durability / High Exploitability

Best targets.

- Rare skill stacks already in use.
- Strong judgment in a valuable arena.
- Short feedback loops attached to real output.
- Reputational or network access that can be used ethically.
- Personal systems that reliably increase throughput or recovery.

### High Durability / Low Exploitability

Latent assets.

- Knowledge or talent without a current arena.
- Genetic or physiological traits with no clear use case.
- Credentials or relationships not connected to a live opportunity.
- Deep interests that have not been converted into production.

Action: find the arena or discard.

### Low Durability / High Exploitability

Tactical edges.

- Temporary information gaps.
- Tooling tricks.
- Short-lived market inefficiencies.
- Low-cost behavioral optimizations.

Action: use if cheap, but do not overbuild around them.

### Low Durability / Low Exploitability

Ignore.

- Identity stories.
- Isolated traits.
- Generic advice.
- Unvalidated biohacks.
- Advantages that require a different life context to matter.

## Implications For Genome Research

Genome data is not automatically an unfair advantage. It becomes relevant only if it reveals, rules out, or sharpens an exploitable asymmetry.

Valid genome-related unfair-advantage paths would need to show:

- the trait or risk is real enough to matter
- the result is actionable
- the action is usable
- the edge is not better discovered by direct measurement
- the benefit exceeds privacy, anxiety, and false-positive costs

Many genome findings will fail this test.

## Better Phase 1 Question

Not: "What SNPs should we inspect?"

Instead:

> What asymmetries can genome data credibly reveal, rule out, or sharpen, and which of those could become exploitable?
